# Checklist workshop: Validating that an objective is fully SMART

THEORETICAL RECAP A SMART goal is not just a well-written sentence — it is a structured commitment that meets five precise criteria. Specific means the goal clearly identifies what needs to be achieved, for whom, and in what context. Measurable means a concrete metric is attached to it, making it possible to track progress and declare success objectively. Achievable means the goal is realistic given the available resources, skills, and constraints. Realistic (also called Relevant) means the goal genuinely contributes to the broader strategic objective — it must matter. Time-Based means a clear deadline or timeframe is defined, creating urgency and a basis for evaluation. A common mistake is to confuse a vague intention with a SMART goal. For example, 'improve our social media presence' is not SMART. A SMART version would be: 'Increase our Instagram account's follower count from 2,000 to 3,500 within 3 months by publishing 4 posts per week targeting 25–35-year-old urban professionals.' Notice how every letter of SMART is addressed. Another frequent error is writing a goal that is Specific and Measurable but not Achievable — for instance, targeting 500% growth in 30 days with no budget. Validity across all five dimensions is non-negotiable.
