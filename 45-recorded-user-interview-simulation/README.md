# Recorded user interview simulation

THEORETICAL RECAP A user interview is a qualitative research method used to gather deep, first-hand insights about a target audience's motivations, frustrations, behaviors, and expectations. Unlike surveys or analytics, interviews let you access the 'why' behind user actions — which is exactly what feeds a solid persona and informs a customer journey map. The key is to ask open-ended, non-leading questions that invite the interviewee to tell stories rather than give yes/no answers. A common mistake is to ask 'Would you use this feature?' (leading, hypothetical) instead of 'Tell me about the last time you tried to solve this problem' (behavioral, retrospective). Another frequent error is jumping straight into product questions without first building rapport and understanding the broader context of the person's life and habits. A well-conducted interview typically lasts 30 to 45 minutes and covers three zones: the person's background and context, their pain points and current behaviors, and their aspirations or workarounds. The output of an interview is not a transcript — it is a set of synthesized insights that directly enrich your persona cards and journey maps. Think of the interview as a bridge: on one side you have hypotheses about your audience; on the other side, you have validated (or invalidated) understanding. The interview is how you cross that bridge. WORKSHOP BRIEF You have now studied user research methods, deep user motivations, persona construction, and audience segmentation.
