# Associate KPIs with each objective and format

THEORETICAL RECAP A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) is a measurable value that tells you whether a specific content objective is being met. The core mental model is simple: every piece of content serves one dominant objective, and every objective has a natural family of KPIs. The classic objective ladder follows the funnel: Awareness (top), Consideration (middle), Conversion (bottom), and Loyalty/Retention (post-purchase). Awareness objectives are measured by reach, impressions, and new visitors. Consideration objectives are measured by time on page, pages per session, video completion rate, and engagement rate. Conversion objectives are measured by conversion rate, leads generated, cost per lead, and sales. Loyalty objectives are measured by returning visitors, repeat purchase rate, and email open/click rates. The format also constrains which KPIs are relevant. A blog article is measured through SEO and dwell-time metrics; a short-form video through view rate and completion; a landing page through conversion rate; a newsletter through open and click-through rates. Common mistakes: (1) attaching vanity metrics (likes, followers) to objectives they cannot prove; (2) using the same KPI for every format regardless of intent; (3) measuring conversion on an awareness asset that was never designed to convert. Mini example: Objective = grow awareness → Format = LinkedIn short video → Primary KPI = video views and reach; Secondary KPI = engagement rate.
