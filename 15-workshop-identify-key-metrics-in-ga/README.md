# Workshop: identify key metrics in GA

THEORETICAL RECAP Google Analytics (GA) is a free web analytics platform that collects and reports data about how users interact with a website. Before diving into the workshop, let's consolidate a few essential concepts. A metric in GA is a quantitative measurement: sessions, users, pageviews, bounce rate, average session duration, goal completions. A dimension is the context that qualifies that metric: source/medium, country, device, landing page. The mental model to hold onto is simple: metrics answer 'how much' or 'how many', while dimensions answer 'who', 'where', 'how', or 'which'. The most common beginner mistake is confusing 'users' and 'sessions'. A single user can generate multiple sessions. Another frequent error is reading the bounce rate in isolation without linking it to the type of page (a blog post naturally has a higher bounce rate than a product page). Finally, beginners often overlook the 'Acquisition' section and jump straight to 'Behavior', missing the crucial traffic source context. Mini example: if you see 5,000 sessions with a 70% bounce rate and an average session duration of 0:45 on your homepage, this suggests visitors are not finding what they need quickly — a signal to investigate the traffic source and the relevance of the landing page content.
