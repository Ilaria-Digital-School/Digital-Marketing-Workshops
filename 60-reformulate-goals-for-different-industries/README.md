# Reformulate goals for different industries

THEORETICAL RECAP A content marketing goal is only actionable when it is SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. You practiced this framework in the previous workshop with a single objective. Now, the challenge is to understand that the same business intent — for example, 'grow our audience' — translates very differently depending on the industry, the target audience, and the available content channels. A vague goal like 'increase brand awareness' is not a content marketing objective: it is a wish. A SMART reformulation would be: 'Publish 4 educational blog posts per month over the next quarter to increase organic traffic by 20% among HR managers aged 30–50.' The key mental model to retain is this: industry context shapes what success looks like. A B2B software company measures goal attainment through lead generation and demo requests. A fashion e-commerce brand measures it through engagement rates and conversion. A nonprofit organization might focus on email list growth and volunteer sign-ups. Common mistakes include copying a goal from one sector and pasting it into another without adapting KPIs, confusing activity metrics (number of posts published) with outcome metrics (audience growth, conversions), and writing goals that are either too broad or too narrow to be useful. Keep this in mind: every reformulation must reflect the specific business reality of the industry you are working with.
