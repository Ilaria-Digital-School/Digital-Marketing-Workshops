# Workshop: Selecting KPIs for an email campaign

**Theoretical Recap** A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) is a measurable value that tells you whether you are making progress toward a specific objective. Unlike a raw metric — which simply describes what happened — a KPI is always tied to a goal and helps you make decisions. In the context of an email campaign, you have access to dozens of metrics (emails sent, bounces, clicks, unsubscribes, etc.), but only a handful of these will qualify as genuine KPIs depending on what you are actually trying to achieve. Remember the four quality criteria for a good KPI: it must be Relevant (aligned with your goal), Available (the data can actually be collected), Reliable (consistent and unbiased), and Actionable (it tells you what to do next). A common mistake is selecting KPIs based on what is easy to measure rather than what matters. For example, tracking 'emails sent' is not a KPI — it is a volume metric with no diagnostic value. Another frequent error is choosing too many KPIs at once, which dilutes focus and makes interpretation harder. Also keep in mind the three KPI families: Acquisition KPIs measure how you reach new contacts (e.g., list growth rate), Engagement KPIs measure how your audience interacts with your content (e.g., open rate, click-through rate), and Conversion KPIs measure whether your audience takes the desired action (e.g., conversion rate, revenue per email).
