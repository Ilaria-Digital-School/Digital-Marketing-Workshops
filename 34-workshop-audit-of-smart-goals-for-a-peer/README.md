# Workshop: Audit of SMART goals for a peer

**Theoretical Recap** A SMART goal is a goal that is Specific (clearly defined, not vague), Measurable (quantifiable with concrete indicators), Achievable (realistic given available resources), Relevant (aligned with broader strategic objectives), and Time-bound (with a clear deadline). In digital marketing, SMART goals are the backbone of any coherent strategy: they ensure that every action can be tracked, evaluated, and adjusted. A common mental model is to think of a SMART goal as a contract between ambition and execution — it forces you to translate intent into a trackable commitment. The most frequent mistakes practitioners make include: setting goals that are too broad ('increase traffic' instead of 'increase organic traffic by 20% in 3 months'), confusing outputs with outcomes (e.g., posting 10 articles vs. generating 500 qualified leads), omitting a KPI entirely, or setting unrealistic targets disconnected from current performance baselines. A solid goal also connects naturally to a KPI and a monitoring frequency — concepts you have already covered in previous sessions. Mini example: 'Grow newsletter subscribers from 1,200 to 1,800 (50% increase) via a lead magnet campaign, by the end of Q3, tracked weekly via the email platform dashboard.' This goal is specific (newsletter subscribers), measurable (1,800 target), achievable (50% growth with a concrete lever), relevant (audience building for a product launch), and time-bound (end of Q3).
