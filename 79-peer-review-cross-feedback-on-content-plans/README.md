# Peer review: cross-feedback on content plans

THEORETICAL RECAP A content plan is the operational document that translates a content strategy into concrete deliverables: topics, formats, channels, publication dates, editorial angles, targeted personas, and the KPIs used to measure success. A strong plan aligns three layers: business objectives (e.g., generate qualified leads), audience needs (what your personas actually search for), and narrative coherence (a storytelling thread that connects individual pieces). The useful mental model is a pyramid: at the top, the strategic objective; in the middle, the content pillars and formats serving that objective; at the base, the individual scheduled items and their assigned KPIs. Peer review is the practice of critically evaluating another learner's plan against these criteria, then articulating specific, actionable feedback. Common mistakes include: producing a calendar without linking each item to a measurable KPI; confusing activity (number of posts) with outcome (conversions, engagement); ignoring the buyer journey so that all content targets awareness and nothing supports decision; and giving vague feedback such as 'nice work' instead of naming what fails and why. A mini example: a plan lists ten blog posts but no nurturing sequence and no distribution channel. Good feedback would state: 'Awareness content is strong, but there is no consideration or decision-stage content and no automation trigger; add a lead magnet plus a three-email nurturing scenario tied to a download event.'
