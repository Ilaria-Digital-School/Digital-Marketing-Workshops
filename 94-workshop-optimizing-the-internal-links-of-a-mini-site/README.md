# Workshop: optimizing the internal links of a mini-site

THEORETICAL RECAP Internal linking (internal mesh) refers to the network of hyperlinks that connect the pages of a single website to one another. Its two main goals are to help users navigate logically and to distribute 'link equity' (often called PageRank or link juice) across your pages, signaling to search engines which pages matter most. The right mental model is to picture your site as a pyramid: the homepage sits at the top, category/pillar pages sit in the middle, and detailed articles sit at the base. Links flow value downward and back upward, while horizontal links connect thematically related content (topic clusters). Anchor text (the clickable words) tells search engines what the target page is about, so it must be descriptive and varied, never generic like 'click here'. Common mistakes include: orphan pages (pages with no incoming internal links), over-optimized exact-match anchors that look spammy, links buried too deep (more than 3 clicks from the homepage), and pointing every link to the homepage while ignoring deep content. A mini example: an article titled 'How to write a Title tag' should link to the pillar page 'On-page SEO guide' using the anchor 'on-page SEO', and receive a link back from that pillar. This creates a bidirectional, semantically coherent relationship that reinforces both pages.
