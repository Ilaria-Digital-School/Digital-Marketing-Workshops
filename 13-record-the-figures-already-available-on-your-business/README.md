# Record the figures already available on your business

Theoretical Recap To make informed marketing decisions, you need a solid foundation of data you already possess. Data, in this context, refers to quantifiable information that describes your business's performance or the behavior of your market. A metric is a specific, measurable data point. A Key Performance Indicator (KPI) is a metric tied to a strategic goal. For a digital marketing expert, constructing a mental model of data flow is essential: sources generate raw data, which you collect and organize; you then analyze it to produce actionable insights for your strategy. Common mistakes include trying to track everything at once, which leads to overwhelm and inaction, and not distinguishing between vanity metrics and actionable ones. A vanity metric looks impressive but doesn't directly inform decisions, like total page views without context. An actionable metric guides specific actions, like the cost per lead from a specific social media ad. Another frequent error is storing data in disparate, unconnected places, making comparison and analysis difficult. Mini example: Imagine a small online craft store. Its available figures might be: Instagram followers (2,500), monthly website visits (3,000), average order value ($45), and the costs of its main marketing channels: Google Ads ($200/month) and Facebook Ads ($150/month). These are the recorded figures. The craft store's owner can then start asking valuable questions, like which channel yields a better return on investment.
