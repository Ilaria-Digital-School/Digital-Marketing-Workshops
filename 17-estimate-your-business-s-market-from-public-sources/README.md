# Estimate your business's market from public sources

You have recently studied why market analysis matters, its key components, and basic sizing methods. This workshop builds directly on that foundation. You will now apply those concepts by estimating a real market using only publicly available information. Begin with a brief theoretical recap to ensure key concepts are fresh in your mind. Theoretical Recap Market estimation is the process of calculating the potential size of a group of customers for a product or service. It converts abstract concepts like 'customers' and 'competitors' into a measurable figure, often expressed as total revenue, number of customers, or transaction volume. This 'market size' is a critical input for any business plan or marketing strategy, as it indicates potential and guides resource allocation. A clear mental model is to distinguish between total addressable market (TAM), serviceable available market (SAM), and serviceable obtainable market (SOM). TAM represents the total revenue opportunity if you captured 100% of the market. SAM is the segment of TAM you can actually reach with your specific products and channels. SOM is the realistic market share you can capture in the short term. Common mistakes include confusing one's own company's potential with the total market size, relying on a single flawed source without cross-referencing, and overestimating the SAM by not applying realistic geographic or demographic constraints.
