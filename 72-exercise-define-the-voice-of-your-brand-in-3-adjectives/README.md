# Exercise: define the voice of your brand in 3 adjectives

THEORETICAL RECAP Brand voice is the consistent personality and tone that a brand expresses across all its content, from blog posts to social media captions. It is not about what you say, but how you say it. A useful mental model: imagine your brand as a person walking into a room. How does that person speak? Are they authoritative and precise, or warm and playful? The three-adjective method forces you to distill this personality into a compact, actionable reference that guides every content decision. For example, a fintech startup might choose 'clear', 'reassuring', and 'bold': 'clear' means short sentences and no jargon; 'reassuring' means acknowledging user concerns; 'bold' means taking a stance on industry issues. Common mistakes to avoid: choosing vague adjectives like 'professional' or 'quality' that apply to almost any brand and give no real direction; picking adjectives that contradict your actual audience or product; and selecting words without defining what they concretely mean in writing. A good adjective must translate into observable writing behavior. If 'friendly' cannot change a single word in your draft, it is decorative, not directive. The strength of this exercise lies in the operational definition you attach to each adjective, turning abstract personality into concrete editorial rules. WORKSHOP BRIEF Your goal in this workshop is to define the brand voice of a real or fictional brand using exactly three adjectives, and to prove that these adjectives are operationally useful.
