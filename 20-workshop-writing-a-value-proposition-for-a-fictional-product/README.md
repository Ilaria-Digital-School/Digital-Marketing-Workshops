# Workshop: writing a value proposition for a fictional product

THEORETICAL RECAP A value proposition is a clear, concise statement that explains how your product or service solves a specific problem, what benefits it delivers, and why a target customer should choose it over any competing alternative. It is not a slogan, not a tagline, and not a list of features. It is a promise of value addressed to a precisely defined audience. The classic mental model to keep in mind: a strong value proposition answers three questions simultaneously — What do you offer? For whom? Why is it better or different? A common mistake is to write something generic ('we offer quality solutions at competitive prices') that could apply to any company in any industry. Another frequent error is confusing the value proposition with the product description: the focus must always be on the customer's benefit, not on the product's technical specifications. A simple example: instead of 'Our app has an AI-powered scheduling engine,' write 'Freelancers save 3 hours per week by letting our app automatically organize their client meetings.' Notice how the benefit is concrete, measurable, and addressed to a specific person. Keep this model in mind as you move into the workshop.
