# Workshop: Diagnosing a non-SMART goal

THEORETICAL RECAP Before diving into the workshop, let's consolidate what you have learned so far. A SMART goal is a goal that meets five criteria: Specific (clearly defined, focused on a precise outcome), Measurable (quantifiable with concrete indicators), Achievable (realistic given your resources and constraints), Relevant (aligned with your broader business strategy), and Time-bound (anchored to a clear deadline). These five criteria work together — a goal that fails even one of them is incomplete and likely to produce confusion, wasted effort, or unmeasurable results. A common mistake among beginners is to confuse a SMART goal with a simple action plan. For example, 'We want to grow our social media presence' is not a goal — it is a vague intention. It is neither measurable nor time-bound, and it lacks specificity. A SMART version would be: 'Increase our Instagram follower count from 2,000 to 5,000 by the end of Q3, by publishing 5 posts per week and running two paid campaigns.' Notice the difference: the second version tells you exactly what success looks like, when it must happen, and gives enough context to evaluate whether it is realistic. Another frequent error is setting goals that are technically SMART but not Relevant — for instance, optimizing click-through rates on a campaign targeting the wrong audience. Always cross-check your goal against the broader strategic context.
