# Workshop: Finding a metric for a goal

THEORETICAL RECAP A goal without a metric is just a wish. In digital marketing, the 'M' in SMART stands for Measurable — and it is one of the most decisive letters in the framework. A metric is a quantifiable indicator that allows you to track progress toward a goal and determine, at a given point in time, whether you have reached it or not. Without a metric, there is no way to evaluate performance, justify decisions, or optimize your actions. The most common mistake beginners make is confusing a metric with a vague result. 'Increase traffic' is not a metric. '+ 30% organic sessions per month on the blog' is. A good metric has three components: a unit of measurement (sessions, leads, conversion rate, revenue...), a target value (30%, 500, x2...), and a time frame (per week, by end of Q3, within 6 months). Another frequent error is choosing a metric that is easy to measure but not relevant — vanity metrics like raw page views or total followers often fall into this trap. Always ask: does this metric directly reflect progress toward my business goal? A quick mental model: Goal → What do I want to achieve? → What concrete, observable change will confirm I achieved it? → How do I measure that change? → That is your metric. Mini example: Goal: 'Generate more newsletter subscribers.' Metric: 'Increase newsletter sign-up rate from 1.2% to 2.5% of blog visitors within 3 months.'
