# Workshop: set up a watch and store the links

**Theoretical Recap** Digital monitoring (or 'veille' in French) is the continuous practice of collecting, filtering, and storing relevant information about your industry, competitors, target audiences, and marketing trends. It is a foundational habit for any digital marketing professional. Two core tools covered in the previous lesson are Google Alerts, which sends email notifications when new content matching your keywords appears on the web, and Feedly, an RSS feed aggregator that centralizes content from blogs, news sites, and online publications in one place. A common mistake beginners make is setting up alerts and feeds but never organizing or storing the links they find. Information collected but not archived becomes useless within days. The correct mental model is: Monitor → Filter → Store → Reuse. Think of your watch system as a living database you feed regularly and consult before any campaign, content creation, or strategic decision. For example, a digital marketer working for an e-commerce brand might set up a Google Alert for 'email marketing trends 2024', subscribe to the Neil Patel blog via Feedly, and save every relevant article into a structured Google Drive folder or a bookmarking tool like Pocket. This creates a personal knowledge base that grows over time and directly informs strategy. --- **Workshop Brief** This workshop is the direct practical application of the lesson on monitoring tools: Google Alerts and Feedly.
