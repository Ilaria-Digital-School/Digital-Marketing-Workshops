# Workshop: drawing an optimized tree structure

THEORETICAL RECAP A site tree structure is the hierarchical organization of your pages, from the homepage down to deep content. Think of it as an inverted pyramid: the homepage sits at the top, main categories form the second level, subcategories the third, and individual pages (articles, products, services) sit at the bottom. A clean mental model is the "three-click rule": any important page should be reachable in three clicks or fewer from the homepage. This structure directly shapes URL design, internal linking, crawl efficiency, and the way link equity (or "PageRank") flows through your site. A well-built tree groups content by semantic theme (siloing), keeps depth shallow, and mirrors real user intent rather than internal company logic. URLs should follow the hierarchy logically, for example: /training/seo/technical-audit. Common mistakes include: creating too many top-level categories (diluting authority), burying key pages too deep, mixing unrelated topics in the same silo, orphan pages with no internal links, and duplicate or overlapping categories that compete for the same keywords. Mini example: for a cooking blog, a poor structure lists 30 flat categories. A better one groups them: Recipes > (Starters, Mains, Desserts), Techniques > (Baking, Grilling), Equipment. Each parent gathers related children, reinforcing topical relevance and simplifying navigation. WORKSHOP BRIEF Your mission is to design, from scratch, an optimized tree structure for a fictional but realistic website of your choice. You may pick an e-commerce shop, a service company, or a content-driven blog, but it must contain at least 25 to 35 distinct pages...
