# Associate types of content to the stages of the journey

**Theoretical Recap** The user journey — also called the customer journey — describes the sequence of stages a potential customer goes through from the moment they first become aware of a problem or need, to the point where they take action and eventually become a loyal advocate. In content marketing, this journey is typically broken down into three core stages: Awareness (the user discovers they have a problem or need), Consideration (they explore possible solutions and compare options), and Decision (they choose a specific solution or provider). Some models add a fourth stage, Loyalty/Advocacy, to account for post-purchase engagement. A common mistake beginners make is producing content that is almost exclusively promotional or product-focused — content that speaks only to people who are already ready to buy. This ignores the vast majority of the audience who are still in earlier stages and need to be educated, inspired, or reassured before they can move forward. Another frequent error is using the same content format for every stage, when in reality, a blog post that raises awareness serves a fundamentally different purpose than a product comparison guide or a customer testimonial. The mental model to retain is simple: match the content type and tone to the mindset of the user at each stage. At Awareness, think educational and broad. At Consideration, think comparative and solution-oriented. At Decision, think reassuring and conversion-focused. At Loyalty, think community and value reinforcement.
