# Peer-to-peer feedback session on the CJM

THEORETICAL RECAP A Customer Journey Map (CJM) is a visual representation of every interaction a persona has with a brand, from the first moment of awareness through to post-purchase loyalty. Its core purpose is to reveal gaps between what the customer expects at each stage and what the brand actually delivers. A well-built CJM combines three essential layers: the stages of the journey (Awareness, Consideration, Decision, Retention, Advocacy), the customer's emotional state and pain points at each stage, and the brand's touchpoints and corresponding KPIs. A common mistake is to build a CJM that reflects internal business logic rather than genuine customer experience — the map must always be anchored in real persona data, not assumptions. Another frequent error is leaving the KPI layer vague: each stage must have at least one measurable indicator (e.g., bounce rate at Awareness, conversion rate at Decision, NPS at Loyalty). Think of the CJM as a diagnostic tool: it does not just describe the journey, it exposes where customers drop off, feel frustrated, or lose trust. A strong CJM directly informs your content strategy, automation scenarios, and optimization priorities — all of which you have already worked on in previous activities. This workshop now asks you to stress-test your own CJM by receiving and giving structured feedback with a peer.
