# Workshop: Customizing labels and deadlines

**Theoretical Recap** In any project management tool like Trello, labels and deadlines are two of the most powerful features for keeping a team — or a solo operator — organized, focused, and on track. A label is a color-coded tag you assign to a card to visually categorize it: by priority (urgent, low), by type of task (content, paid ads, email, social media), or by status (to review, approved, blocked). Labels work as a visual filtering system — at a glance, you can scan your board and instantly understand what kind of work is pending. A deadline (or due date in Trello) is a timestamp attached to a card that triggers visual alerts as the date approaches or passes. Cards turn yellow when a deadline is near and red when overdue. A common mistake beginners make is creating too many labels with no consistent logic, making the board harder to read rather than easier. Another frequent error is setting deadlines without reviewing them regularly — a missed deadline that stays red on the board for days signals poor project hygiene. The best practice is to define your label taxonomy before you start, keep it to 5–7 max, and assign deadlines only to actionable tasks, not ideas or backlog items. Example: On a Digital Campaign board, you might use labels like: 🔴 Urgent, 🟡 Content, 🔵 Paid Media, 🟢 Done/Validated, 🟠 Blocked. Each card for writing an Instagram post gets the 🟡 Content label and a deadline matching your editorial calendar....
