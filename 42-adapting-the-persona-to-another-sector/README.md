# Adapting the persona to another sector

THEORETICAL RECAP A persona is a semi-fictional representation of a typical customer segment, built from real data (interviews, analytics, surveys) and structured assumptions. It goes beyond demographic data by incorporating psychographic elements (motivations, fears, values) and behavioral patterns (purchase habits, preferred channels, decision triggers). The core purpose of a persona is to make abstract audience segments tangible, so that every marketing decision — from content tone to channel choice — is grounded in genuine human understanding. A critical skill, often underestimated by beginners, is the ability to transfer and adapt a persona across sectors. The underlying framework remains constant: you are still mapping a human being's goals, frustrations, context, and decision-making process. What changes is the vocabulary, the pain points, the touchpoints, and the stakes. A persona built for a B2C e-commerce fashion brand will share structural DNA with one built for a B2B SaaS tool — but the motivations, the buying cycle, and the language will differ profoundly. Common mistakes when adapting a persona: copying demographic data without questioning whether it holds in the new context; ignoring sector-specific constraints (budget cycles, regulatory environment, decision-making units); and failing to update the channels and touchpoints to reflect where the new sector's audience actually lives online.
