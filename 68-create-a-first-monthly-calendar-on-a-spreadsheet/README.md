# Create a first monthly calendar on a spreadsheet

THEORETICAL RECAP An editorial calendar is a planning tool that maps out what content you publish, when, where, and why. Think of it as a mental model shaped like a grid: rows represent individual content pieces, and columns represent the decisions attached to each piece (date, channel, format, topic, target persona, narrative angle, status). A good calendar transforms a vague intention ("I should post more") into a concrete, repeatable operational plan. Why it matters: content marketing only produces results with consistency and alignment. Your calendar connects three things you already studied: your personas (who you talk to), your storytelling structure (hero, conflict, resolution), and your business goals (awareness, engagement, conversion). Each planned post should serve a specific persona and carry a clear narrative angle. Common mistakes: planning topics with no link to a persona or goal; overloading the calendar with an unrealistic publishing rhythm; forgetting a "status" column so you never know what is drafted, scheduled, or published; and mixing channels without adapting the format. Mini example: a single row might read — Date: March 4 | Channel: LinkedIn | Format: carousel | Persona: junior marketer | Angle: "the conflict of imposter syndrome" | Goal: engagement | Status: To write. That one line tells you exactly what to produce and why. WORKSHOP BRIEF Your mission is to build a functional monthly editorial calendar in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel, or any equivalent).
