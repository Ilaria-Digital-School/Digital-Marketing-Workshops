# Trace your own journey on a recent purchase

Theoretical Recap: The Customer Journey Touchpoint Map Understanding the customer journey is fundamental to digital marketing. It represents the complete sequence of interactions, or touchpoints, a person has with a brand across different channels, from initial discovery through to purchase and beyond. This journey is rarely linear, often looping back through awareness or consideration stages. A key mental model is the touchpoint map. This visual tool logs every specific interaction a customer has with marketing stimuli. It separates channels (like social media or search engines) from actions (like clicking an ad or reading a review). Mapping this reveals how free and paid channels work together to guide decisions. Common mistakes include assuming a simple, direct path or focusing only on the final conversion. Marketers often overlook micro-moments, like a quick price check, or the role of offline influences. Another error is failing to identify which touchpoints are owned (your website), earned (PR), or paid (ads), which impacts cost analysis. For a mini-example, consider buying running shoes. The journey might start with an Instagram post (paid social), lead to a Google search for reviews (organic search), then a visit to a retailer's website (owned channel) to check stock. An abandoned cart might trigger a retargeting ad (paid display) later, culminating in an in-store purchase and a post-purchase email (owned channel). Each step is a distinct touchpoint.
