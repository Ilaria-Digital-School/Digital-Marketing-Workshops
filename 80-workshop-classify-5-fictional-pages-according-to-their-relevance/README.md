# Workshop: classify 5 fictional pages according to their relevance

THEORETICAL RECAP Relevance is the degree to which a page answers the intent behind a user's query. A search engine ranks pages by combining several signals: keyword alignment with the query, quality and depth of content, page structure (titles, headings, metadata), user experience (loading speed, mobile-friendliness), authority (backlinks, domain trust), and freshness. A useful mental model: imagine the search engine as a librarian who must hand a visitor the single best book for their exact question. The librarian does not pick the thickest book, nor the one with the flashiest cover; they pick the one whose content most precisely matches what the visitor asked, written by a trustworthy author, and easy to read. Common mistakes to avoid: confusing keyword stuffing with relevance (repeating a term does not make a page more useful), ignoring search intent (a page optimized for 'buy running shoes' should not target someone researching 'how to run a marathon'), and overvaluing design over substance. Another frequent error is forgetting that relevance is always relative to a specific query, not an absolute property of the page. Mini example: for the query 'best budget smartphones 2024', a well-structured comparison article with recent data, clear headings, and reputable sourcing is more relevant than a single product sales page or an outdated 2019 listicle.
