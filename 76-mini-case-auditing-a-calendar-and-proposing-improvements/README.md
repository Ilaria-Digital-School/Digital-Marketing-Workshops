# Mini-case: auditing a calendar and proposing improvements

THEORETICAL RECAP A content calendar (editorial calendar) is the operational document that plans what you publish, when, on which channel, for which audience, and toward which goal. Auditing a calendar means evaluating whether these planned publications actually serve a coherent strategy: alignment with business objectives, coverage of the customer journey (awareness, consideration, decision, loyalty), respect for the brand voice, balance across formats (blog, video, podcast, infographic), and realistic workload distribution over time. A useful mental model is the three-layer check: STRATEGY (does each piece map to a goal and a journey stage?), BALANCE (are formats, topics, and channels varied and evenly loaded?), and EXECUTION (are frequency, deadlines, and validation workflow sustainable?). Audit from top to bottom: a beautifully produced video is worthless if it targets no clear stage of the journey. Common mistakes: overloading certain weeks while leaving others empty; publishing only bottom-of-funnel promotional content; mixing brand voices; ignoring recycling opportunities; and having no validation process, which causes bottlenecks. Mini example: a calendar posts five promotional articles in one week, then nothing for two weeks. The audit reveals no awareness-stage content, an overloaded calendar (as seen in your rebalancing lesson), and a missed chance to recycle a webinar into a blog post plus three social snippets. The fix: redistribute publications, add top-of-funnel formats, and recycle existing assets.
