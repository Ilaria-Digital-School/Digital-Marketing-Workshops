# Workshop: create a basic persona

THEORETICAL RECAP A persona is a semi-fictional representation of your ideal audience member, built from real data, behavioral observations, and educated assumptions. It is not a vague archetype or a demographic statistic — it is a concrete, named character that embodies the motivations, frustrations, habits, and goals of a specific segment of your target audience. The purpose of a persona is to make strategic decisions more human: instead of producing content for 'adults aged 25–40,' you produce it for 'Laura, a 32-year-old project manager who needs to upskill fast and consumes content during her commute.' A well-built persona always includes at minimum: a name and a profile summary, socio-demographic information, professional context, key goals and motivations, main pain points and frustrations, content consumption habits (platforms, formats, frequency), and a direct quote that captures how this person thinks or speaks. Common mistakes to avoid: creating personas based purely on intuition without any real data, making them too broad or too numerous (one or two sharp personas beat five vague ones), or forgetting to connect the persona to actual content decisions. A persona is a tool, not a decoration — if it doesn't inform what you publish, when, and how, it isn't doing its job.
