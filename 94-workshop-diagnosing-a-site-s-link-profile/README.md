# Workshop: diagnosing a site's link profile

THEORETICAL RECAP A site's link profile is the complete set of external links (backlinks) pointing to a domain, plus how they interconnect internally. It is one of the strongest off-page signals search engines use to evaluate authority and trust. Three concepts matter most. First, quantity vs. quality: ten links from authoritative, topically relevant sites usually outweigh hundreds from low-value directories or spammy networks. Second, anchor text distribution: the clickable words used in incoming links. A healthy profile shows natural variety (branded, generic, URL, partial-match) rather than an over-optimized flood of exact-match commercial keywords. Third, link attributes: 'dofollow' links pass authority, while 'nofollow', 'sponsored', and 'ugc' generally do not transmit the same value. A useful mental model: picture your site as a candidate for a job. Backlinks are recommendation letters. Who writes them (domain authority), how relevant their expertise is (topical relevance), and whether the wording sounds authentic (anchor text) all shape credibility. Common mistakes: buying bulk links, chasing raw volume, ignoring toxic links, and using identical anchor text repeatedly. These trigger penalties or dilute results. Mini example: a local bakery earns a backlink from a regional food blog (relevant, dofollow, branded anchor 'Chez Marie bakery').
