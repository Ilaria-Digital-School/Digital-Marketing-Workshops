# Workshop: quick audit of existing content

THEORETICAL RECAP A content audit is the systematic review of existing pages to measure how well they serve both users and search engines. The core mental model is simple: every page should answer a clear search intent, target a defined keyword, and demonstrate expertise, experience, authoritativeness and trustworthiness (E-E-A-T). A 'quick audit' does not rewrite everything; it diagnoses gaps against the on-page fundamentals you already studied: the Title tag, the Meta Description, the Hn hierarchy (one H1, logical H2/H3), keyword placement, and content quality signals. Think of each page as a contract between a query and a promise. If the Title promises one thing but the body delivers another, intent is broken and rankings suffer. Common mistakes include: multiple H1s or skipped heading levels, Titles over 60 characters that get truncated in the SERP, Meta Descriptions that are missing or duplicated, keyword stuffing instead of natural relevance, and thin content with no author signals or sources. Mini example: a page titled 'Home' targeting 'affordable running shoes' fails immediately. A better Title is 'Affordable Running Shoes for Beginners | Under $60', with an H1 that echoes the intent, H2s covering price, comfort and reviews, and a concise Meta Description including the keyword and a call to action. That single fix aligns intent, keyword and structure in one move.
