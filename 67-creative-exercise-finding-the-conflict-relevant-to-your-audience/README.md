# Creative exercise: finding the conflict relevant to your audience

THEORETICAL RECAP In storytelling, conflict is not about drama for its own sake — it is the engine of every compelling narrative. A conflict is the gap between where your audience currently is (their pain point, frustration, or unfulfilled desire) and where they want to be (their goal, aspiration, or solution). Without this gap, there is no tension, and without tension, there is no reason for an audience to keep reading, watching, or listening. A common mistake among beginners is confusing the product or service with the conflict. Your brand is not the conflict — it is the resolution. The conflict belongs entirely to your audience, expressed through their fears, blockers, doubts, or unmet needs. Another frequent error is choosing a conflict that is too generic ('I want to be more successful') rather than one that is specific, felt, and emotionally resonant for a precise persona. The mental model to keep in mind: Conflict = Desired State − Current Reality. The sharper and more honest this gap is, the more your audience will feel seen and the more powerful your story becomes. Mini example: A freelance graphic designer struggles to find stable clients (current reality). She wants to build a recurring income from meaningful projects (desired state). The conflict: she has the skills but not the visibility or the positioning.
