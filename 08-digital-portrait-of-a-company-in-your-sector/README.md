# Digital portrait of a company in your sector

Recap what a digital portrait looks at A digital portrait is a structured snapshot of how a company shows up online at a given moment. It answers three questions you have already worked on. Which channels does the company use? At which stage of the customer journey (attract, convert, retain) does each channel act? Does the company's online presence give customers what they look for, namely information, reviews, prices and contact details? The mental model is simple. Put yourself in a customer's shoes and follow the journey from the first search to the repeat purchase. At each stage, note what helps you move forward and what blocks you. Then attach a number to each observation, because digital is steered with figures, not impressions. Common mistakes include listing channels without linking them to a stage and judging a website on its looks rather than on what the customer needs. Learners also confuse activity, such as posting a lot, with results. Writing opinions without evidence is another frequent trap. Here is a mini example with an imaginary bakery. It has a Google Business Profile rated 4.3 out of 5 from 58 reviews, an Instagram account with 1,200 followers but no post for 4 months, and a website with no opening hours. The attract stage works, the retain stage is dormant, and the missing hours create friction at the convert stage. That gives you three facts, three figures and one clear diagnosis.
