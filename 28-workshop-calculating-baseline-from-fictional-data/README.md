# Workshop: Calculating baseline from fictional data

**Theoretical Recap** A baseline is the reference measurement of a KPI captured at a specific point in time, before any new action or campaign is launched. It answers a simple question: 'Where are we starting from?' Without a reliable baseline, setting a SMART target becomes guesswork — you cannot define what 'better' means if you do not know where you currently stand. A baseline must come from real, verifiable historical data (analytics exports, CRM reports, ad platform dashboards) and should ideally cover a representative time window — typically the last 30, 60, or 90 days depending on the volume of activity. A common mistake is confusing a baseline with a goal. The baseline is neutral — it simply describes current reality. Another frequent error is calculating the baseline over an atypical period (a promotional spike, a seasonal low, a technical incident), which produces a distorted reference and leads to unrealistic targets. Always sanity-check your data before locking in a baseline. Mental model: Baseline → Gap analysis → SMART Target → KPI monitoring. The baseline anchors the entire chain. For example, if your email open rate over the last 60 days averaged 18.4%, that figure is your baseline. From there, you can set a SMART target such as 'Reach a 22% open rate within 3 months by optimizing subject lines and send-time personalization.' --- **Workshop Brief** This workshop directly follows the theory lesson on defining a baseline and a target.
