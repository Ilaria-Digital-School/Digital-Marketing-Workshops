# Choose the format adapted to each stage of the journey

THEORETICAL RECAP The customer journey describes the path a prospect follows from first contact to loyal customer. It is commonly divided into three stages: awareness (the person discovers a problem or need), consideration (they compare solutions and evaluate options), and decision (they choose and buy). A useful mental model is a funnel: broad and educational at the top, specific and reassuring at the bottom. Each stage requires a different content format because the audience's intent changes. At the awareness stage, formats such as blog articles, infographics, short videos, and podcasts work well: they attract, educate, and build trust without pushing a sale. At the consideration stage, comparative guides, webinars, case studies, and detailed how-to videos help the prospect weigh their options. At the decision stage, product demos, customer testimonials, free trials, and pricing pages remove the final doubts. Common mistakes: pushing a hard sales message during the awareness stage, using a single format everywhere, or ignoring the reader's intent. Mini example: a SaaS company publishes a blog post "5 signs your team wastes time on manual tasks" (awareness), then a comparison guide of automation tools (consideration), then a case study with measurable results plus a free trial CTA (decision). Each format matches the mindset of the prospect at that moment, which increases conversion and reduces friction.
