# Workshop: adding emotions and expectations to the CJM

THEORETICAL RECAP A Customer Journey Map (CJM) is only truly operational when it goes beyond listing steps and touchpoints. The emotional layer is what transforms a functional diagram into a genuine strategic tool. Emotions refer to how a customer feels at each stage of their journey — frustration, excitement, confusion, relief, trust — while expectations describe what they hope to experience, receive, or resolve at that same moment. These two dimensions are distinct: a customer can expect a quick response and still feel frustrated if the tone of that response feels cold. Confusing the two is a common mistake. Another frequent error is projecting your own feelings onto the customer rather than grounding emotions in real behavioral or qualitative data. A useful mental model: think of the emotional layer as a heartbeat line running across your CJM. When it dips, you are likely facing a pain point or unmet expectation. When it peaks, you have identified a moment of delight worth amplifying. Expectations, on the other hand, function as an invisible contract the customer brings into each stage. If that contract is broken, trust erodes. If it is exceeded, loyalty is built. Mini example: a user lands on an e-commerce site (awareness stage). Their expectation is to quickly find a product that matches their need. If navigation is unclear, their emotion shifts to confusion and mild frustration. Mapping both elements at this step immediately reveals where UX improvements or reassurance mechanisms (filters, search bar, social proof) should be prioritized. ---...
