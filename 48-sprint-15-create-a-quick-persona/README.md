# Sprint 15': create a quick persona

THEORETICAL RECAP A persona is a fictional but realistic archetype of a user segment, built from real qualitative and quantitative data. Unlike a market segment — which is a statistical group defined by shared criteria (age, income, behavior) — a persona gives that group a human face: a name, a story, motivations, frustrations, and goals. The persona does not replace segmentation; it translates it into actionable empathy. A common mistake is to build a persona from assumptions or stereotypes rather than actual research. Another frequent error is creating too many personas (more than 3–4 for a given project), which dilutes focus and makes decision-making harder. A good persona answers three core questions: Who is this person really? What do they want to achieve? What is stopping them? For example, a SaaS company targeting SME owners might define 'Marc, 42, managing director of a 15-person logistics firm, who wants to digitize his invoicing but fears losing control over his data.' That one sentence already guides tone of voice, channel choice, and content strategy. Keep in mind: a persona is only as strong as the data behind it. Validation through interviews, analytics, and behavioral data is what separates a useful persona from a marketing fiction.
