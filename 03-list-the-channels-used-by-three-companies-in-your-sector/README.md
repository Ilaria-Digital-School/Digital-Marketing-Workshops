# List the channels used by three companies in your sector

Recap: Understanding Marketing Channels A marketing channel is a system or medium through which a company communicates with, engages, and delivers value to its target audience, with the ultimate goal of achieving a commercial objective. In digital marketing, channels are divided into owned, earned, and paid media. Owned channels are platforms you fully control, like your website or email list. Earned channels result from others promoting your brand, such as organic social shares or press mentions. Paid channels require a budget to reach an audience, like search engine ads or influencer partnerships. A clear mental model is to view channels as the touchpoints in a customer journey, from first awareness to final purchase and loyalty. Common mistakes include listing every possible channel without strategic thought, confusing channels with tactics (e.g., listing 'videos' instead of 'YouTube'), and failing to consider how channels integrate. A brand using only paid channels may lack long-term sustainability, while one using only organic channels may grow too slowly. A robust strategy typically blends different types. Consider a local bakery aiming to increase online cake orders by 20%. Their channel mix might include: their owned website for orders, paid Google Ads targeting 'birthday cakes near me', and earned media through customer reviews on Google My Business. They complement this with owned email newsletters to past customers and paid promotions on Instagram to reach a younger audience.
