# Adapting existing content to a new persona

THEORETICAL RECAP Adapting content to a persona means reworking an existing piece of content — its tone, angle, vocabulary, format, or depth — so that it resonates specifically with a defined audience profile. A persona is a semi-fictional representation of your ideal reader or customer, built from demographic data, behavioral patterns, motivations, pain points, and goals. Content that works perfectly for a 45-year-old HR director will likely fall flat for a 28-year-old freelance graphic designer, even if the core topic is identical. The message, the framing, and the call to action all need to shift. A clear mental model: think of your original content as a raw ingredient. The persona is the recipe. The same tomato can become a gazpacho, a pasta sauce, or a bruschetta topping depending on who you are cooking for. Your job is not to rewrite from scratch — it is to reframe, reword, and restructure what already exists to serve a new audience. Common mistakes to avoid: changing everything when only the angle needs shifting; ignoring the persona's stage in the user journey; writing in a generic tone that speaks to no one; or forgetting to adjust the call to action to match the persona's actual goals. Mini example: a blog post titled 'How to manage your budget' written for a young entrepreneur could be adapted for a retiree by keeping the financial advice but replacing startup examples with retirement income management scenarios, softening the urgency, and focusing on stability rather than growth. --- WORKSHOP...
