# Workshop: Classifying KPIs by family

THEORETICAL RECAP Before diving into the workshop, let's consolidate the essential concepts covered in the previous lesson on the three families of KPIs. A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) is a measurable value that tells you whether a specific digital marketing objective is being met. Unlike a raw metric — which is simply a data point — a KPI is always tied to a strategic goal. The three families of KPIs in digital marketing are: Acquisition KPIs (how users find you), Engagement KPIs (how users interact with your content), and Conversion KPIs (how users take the desired action). Acquisition KPIs include indicators like organic traffic, cost per click (CPC), number of new visitors, or click-through rate (CTR) on ads. Engagement KPIs cover time on page, bounce rate, pages per session, social media shares, or email open rate. Conversion KPIs track actions that generate value: conversion rate, number of leads generated, cost per acquisition (CPA), or revenue per user. A common mistake is confusing engagement and conversion: a user who reads three pages of your site is engaged, but only converts when they fill in a contact form or make a purchase. Another frequent error is treating all metrics as KPIs — remember, a KPI must be directly linked to a SMART goal.
