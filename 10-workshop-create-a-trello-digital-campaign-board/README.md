# Workshop: create a Trello 'Digital Campaign' board

THEORETICAL RECAP Trello is a visual project management tool based on the Kanban method. The core principle is simple: work moves through columns (called lists) from left to right, representing stages of progress. Each unit of work is a card, which can contain descriptions, checklists, due dates, attachments, labels, and assigned members. A board is the container for an entire project or workflow. In a digital marketing context, a Trello board is not just an organizer — it is a strategic command center. It lets you track campaign tasks, assign responsibilities, set deadlines, and maintain a clear overview of what is planned, in progress, and done. The most common mistakes beginners make are: creating too many lists (which fragments the flow), leaving cards with no due dates or owners (which kills accountability), and mixing strategic tasks with operational micro-tasks on the same level (which creates noise). A simple mental model: think of your board as a newspaper front page. At a glance, anyone — you, a client, a colleague — should understand what the campaign is about, what stage it is at, and what needs immediate attention. A well-structured Trello board achieves exactly that. Mini example: a social media campaign board might have lists titled 'Backlog', 'In Progress', 'Under Review', 'Published', and 'Archived'.
