# Customer journey from another sector

THEORETICAL RECAP A customer journey map is a visual or narrative representation of every step a customer takes when interacting with a brand, from the first moment of awareness through purchase, use, and beyond. It is built around stages (typically: Awareness, Consideration, Decision, Retention, Advocacy), touchpoints (every channel or moment where the customer and brand make contact), and the emotional state of the customer at each step. The key mental model to retain is this: the journey is always told from the customer's perspective, not the company's. A common beginner mistake is to map what the company does rather than what the customer experiences and feels. Another frequent error is to confuse touchpoints with stages — a touchpoint is a specific interaction (a Google ad, a product page, a confirmation email), while a stage is the broader phase the customer is in. Mini example: a person looking for a new gym membership first sees an Instagram ad (touchpoint, Awareness stage), then reads Google reviews (touchpoint, Consideration stage), then visits the gym website to check pricing (touchpoint, Decision stage). Each touchpoint triggers an emotion and either moves the customer forward or creates friction. Mapping these moments in a sector you have not worked in before forces you to reason from data and empathy rather than assumptions — which is precisely the skill this workshop trains.
