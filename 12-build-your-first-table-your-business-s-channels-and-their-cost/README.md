# Build your first table: your business's channels and their cost

Theoretical recap: Channels and Costs A marketing channel is any medium you use to communicate with or reach your audience and customers. This includes owned channels like your website, earned channels like press mentions, and paid channels like social media advertising. A key tool for organising this knowledge is a channel table, which provides a centralised, clear view of your marketing ecosystem and its associated investments. The mental model here is simple: you must first identify what you are already doing, then systematically quantify its direct financial cost and the time investment required. Common mistakes include forgetting hidden costs like employee time or software subscriptions, mixing up one-off and recurring expenses, and being vague. Precision is your goal for effective future analysis and budget allocation. As a mini example, consider a small business using Instagram. You would list Instagram as a channel. The monthly costs might include $50 for boosted posts and a $15 Canva Pro subscription for content creation. The estimated monthly time investment could be 10 hours for community management and content planning, valued at a $30 hourly rate, adding a $300 time cost. Define your scope and gather data Your first task is to define the scope of this analysis. Decide on a specific time period for your cost assessment, such as the last quarter or the upcoming month.
