# Workshop: Mapping KPI to the AARRR funnel

**Theoretical Recap** The AARRR funnel — also known as the Pirate Metrics framework, coined by Dave McClure — is a five-stage model that maps the full lifecycle of a customer's relationship with a digital product or service: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, and Revenue. Each stage describes a distinct behavioral milestone, and each requires its own set of KPIs to be properly monitored. Acquisition measures how users find you (e.g., traffic sources, Cost Per Click, organic sessions). Activation measures the quality of the first experience (e.g., bounce rate, time on site, account creation rate). Retention tracks whether users come back (e.g., return visit rate, email open rate, DAU/MAU ratio). Referral evaluates whether satisfied users recommend you (e.g., Net Promoter Score, referral traffic, viral coefficient). Revenue measures monetization (e.g., conversion rate, Average Order Value, Customer Lifetime Value). A common mistake is assigning the same KPIs to multiple stages without distinguishing their diagnostic purpose. For example, the conversion rate can appear at both Activation and Revenue, but it measures very different behaviors at each stage. Another frequent error is selecting KPIs based on availability rather than relevance — just because a metric is easy to pull from Google Analytics does not mean it answers the right strategic question. Mini example: A SaaS startup notices strong Acquisition numbers (high traffic) but poor Activation (low free trial sign-ups).
