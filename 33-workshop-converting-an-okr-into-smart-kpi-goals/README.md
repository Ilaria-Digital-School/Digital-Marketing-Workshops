# Workshop: Converting an OKR into SMART + KPI goals

THEORETICAL RECAP Before diving into the workshop, let's consolidate the essential concepts you need to carry out this exercise confidently. An OKR (Objective and Key Result) is a strategic framework composed of two elements: a qualitative, inspiring Objective that sets a direction ('Become the leading e-commerce player in our niche'), and measurable Key Results that define what success looks like ('Achieve a 30% increase in organic traffic by Q4'). OKRs are typically ambitious, sometimes stretch goals that operate at a high strategic level. A SMART goal, by contrast, is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. Where OKRs set the vision, SMART goals operationalize it into executable, realistic commitments. A SMART goal derived from the OKR above might read: 'Increase organic traffic by 20% over the next 3 months by publishing 8 SEO-optimized articles per month targeting high-volume keywords in our product category.' A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) is the metric you monitor to assess whether your SMART goal is being reached. It is not the goal itself — it is the measurement instrument. For the example above, relevant KPIs would include: number of organic sessions per week, keyword ranking evolution, and bounce rate on landing pages. Common mistakes to avoid: confusing the OKR Key Result with the SMART goal (they operate at different levels of granularity); choosing vanity KPIs (e.g., total page views) instead of actionable ones; or writing SMART goals that are vague on the 'how' dimension. Always ask: is this measurable, realistic, and time-bound enough to be tracked...
