# Mini-case: diagnosis of a beginner content strategy

THEORETICAL RECAP A content strategy is not simply a list of articles to publish or social media posts to schedule. It is a coherent system that connects three pillars: a clearly defined objective, a precise understanding of the target audience (persona), and a mapping of the content to the stages of the user journey. When one of these pillars is missing or misaligned, the strategy loses effectiveness — even if the content produced is of good quality. A common mistake among beginners is to create content based on what the brand wants to say, rather than on what the audience needs to hear at a given moment in their journey. Another frequent error is to treat all personas the same way, ignoring the fact that a person in the awareness stage does not need the same type of content as someone already in the decision stage. Here is a simple mental model to keep in mind: Objective → Persona → Journey Stage → Content Type. Every piece of content should be traceable back through this chain. If it cannot be, it is likely misaligned. Mini example: A SaaS startup publishes only product comparison articles. Their persona is a junior marketing manager at the awareness stage. The mismatch is clear — comparison content serves the decision stage, not awareness. The fix would be to produce educational content (guides, explainer posts) before introducing comparisons.
