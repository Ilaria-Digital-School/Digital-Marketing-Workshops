# Turning a sales story into a customer story

THEORETICAL RECAP A sales story is brand-centric: it showcases a product, its features, its price, its superiority over competitors. It speaks from the company's perspective and answers the question 'Why should you buy from us?' A customer story, by contrast, is human-centric: it places a real person (or a representative persona) at the center of the narrative, follows their journey through a problem, a transformation, and a resolution, and answers the question 'How did someone like you overcome a challenge using this solution?' The brand becomes a guide or a tool, not the hero. The key mental model to retain: in a customer story, the customer is always the protagonist. The brand is the mentor (think Yoda, not Luke Skywalker). This shift in perspective is not cosmetic — it completely changes the tone, the structure, the vocabulary, and the emotional impact of the content. Common mistakes to avoid: keeping the brand as the main character even while pretending to tell a customer story; listing product features disguised as benefits; using abstract language instead of concrete, sensory details; skipping the conflict or struggle (without tension, there is no story); and ending on the product rather than on the customer's transformation.
