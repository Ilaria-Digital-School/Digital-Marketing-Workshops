# List the changes affecting your market

Theoretical Recap Analysing market changes requires you to track evolving forces that affect demand, competition, and business conditions. This process is called environmental scanning. A market is not static; it is shaped by continuous shifts that create opportunities and threats. Your goal is to identify and document these shifts systematically. Key definitions include trends, which are long-term directional shifts in behaviour or technology, and disruptions, which are sudden changes that alter market rules. A common mental model is to categorise changes using a framework like PESTEL, which examines Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, and Legal factors. This structure ensures a comprehensive scan. A frequent mistake is focusing only on obvious technological changes while ignoring subtle social or regulatory shifts. Another error is confusing a temporary fad with a genuine trend. You must look for evidence of sustained impact. Avoid vague statements; quantify changes where possible. Consider a small example: a local bakery's market. A social change might be a 20% annual increase in demand for gluten-free products over three years. An economic change could be a 15% rise in flour costs due to international supply issues. A technological change is the adoption of delivery app integrations by 60% of competing bakeries.
