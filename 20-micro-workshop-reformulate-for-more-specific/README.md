# Micro-workshop: Reformulate for more Specific

THEORETICAL RECAP The 'S' in SMART stands for Specific — and it is the foundation everything else rests on. A goal is Specific when it answers, without ambiguity, the following questions: What exactly do I want to achieve? Who is involved? On which channel or product? Why does this goal matter? A vague goal like 'increase traffic' gives you nothing to act on. A Specific goal — 'increase organic traffic to the blog section of our e-commerce website by attracting new visitors aged 25–40 interested in sustainable fashion' — tells you where to act, for whom, and through what lever. The most common mistake beginners make is confusing a direction with a destination. 'Improve our social media presence' is a direction. It lacks a defined target, a named channel, a specific audience segment, and a clear outcome. Another frequent error is being specific about the metric only (e.g., '10,000 visits') while leaving the audience, channel, or product scope undefined. Specificity is not just about numbers — it is about clarity of context. A useful mental model: imagine handing your goal to a colleague who knows nothing about your project. Could they immediately understand what needs to be done, for whom, and on which channel? If they need to ask follow-up questions, your goal is not Specific enough. Mini example — Before: 'Get more leads.' After: 'Generate qualified leads from small business owners in France via LinkedIn Ads campaigns promoting our accounting software free trial.'
