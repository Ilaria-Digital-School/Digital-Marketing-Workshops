# Rebalancing an overloaded calendar

THEORETICAL RECAP An editorial calendar is a planning document that maps what content you publish, on which format, on which channel, and when. In previous lessons you learned its role, best practices, how to align content with goals, and how to match formats (blog, video, podcast, infographics) to each stage of the customer journey. An overloaded calendar happens when the volume or complexity of planned content exceeds available time, budget, or team capacity. The mental model to keep in mind is a scale: on one side sit your resources (hours, skills, budget), on the other your commitments (number of pieces, production effort per format). When the scale tips, quality drops, deadlines slip, and consistency breaks. Common mistakes: planning too many high-effort formats (video, long-form) in the same week; ignoring repurposing opportunities; treating every piece as equally strategic; and forgetting buffer time for review and promotion. A healthy calendar prioritizes pieces tied to concrete goals and journey stages, then spreads production load evenly. Mini example: a solo marketer plans 4 blog posts, 2 videos, and 1 podcast per week. That is unrealistic. Rebalanced, they keep 1 blog post per week, publish 1 video every two weeks, and repurpose each blog into an infographic. Same strategic coverage, sustainable workload. WORKSHOP BRIEF Your task is to diagnose and rebalance a deliberately overloaded monthly editorial calendar so it becomes realistic, goal-aligned, and journey-consistent.
