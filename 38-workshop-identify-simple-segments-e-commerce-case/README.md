# Workshop: identify simple segments (e-commerce case)

THEORETICAL RECAP Audience segmentation means dividing a broad market into distinct subgroups of consumers who share common characteristics, so that marketing messages, offers, and channels can be tailored to each group. The four main segmentation criteria you have already studied are: demographic (age, gender, income, family status), psychographic (values, lifestyle, personality, motivations), behavioral (purchase frequency, loyalty, usage patterns, benefits sought), and geographic (country, region, urban vs. rural). A solid segment must meet four conditions: it must be measurable, substantial enough to be worth targeting, accessible through available channels, and actionable with realistic marketing efforts. A common mistake beginners make is confusing a segment with a persona. A segment is a group defined by shared criteria; a persona is a fictional, humanized profile built from a segment. Another frequent error is over-segmenting: creating too many micro-segments makes execution impossible. Start broad, then refine. Mini-example: An online sports equipment store notices that 40% of its customers are women aged 25–40 who buy yoga and pilates gear in the evening on mobile. That behavioral and demographic overlap forms a coherent, actionable segment — different from male customers aged 30–50 who buy running or cycling gear on desktop during lunch breaks. These two segments need different visuals, copy, timing, and channel strategies. Keep this mental model in mind: segmentation is not about describing everyone — it is about finding the meaningful differences that justify different marketing treatments. --- WORKSHOP BRIEF You are now going to apply segmentation directly to a realistic e-commerce scenario.
