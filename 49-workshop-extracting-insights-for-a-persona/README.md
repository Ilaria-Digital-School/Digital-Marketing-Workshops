# Workshop: extracting insights for a persona

**Theoretical Recap** A persona is a semi-fictional representation of your ideal customer, built from real data and validated assumptions. It goes far beyond a simple demographic profile: it combines behavioral patterns, motivations, frustrations, goals, and decision-making triggers. The key mental model to keep in mind is this — a persona is not invented, it is extracted. You synthesize signals from multiple sources (interviews, analytics, reviews, social listening) into a coherent, humanized profile that guides marketing decisions. The three most common mistakes beginners make are: (1) building personas based on intuition rather than data, (2) creating too many personas and losing focus, and (3) confusing a segment with a persona. A segment is a group defined by shared criteria (e.g., women aged 25–34 interested in fitness). A persona is the embodiment of that segment — one named individual with a story, a daily routine, and specific pain points. For example, 'Active urban millennials' is a segment. 'Clara, 29, marketing manager in Lyon, who runs three times a week but struggles to find time for meal prep' is a persona. Extracting insights means identifying the recurring patterns, tensions, and motivations that emerge from your data sources.
