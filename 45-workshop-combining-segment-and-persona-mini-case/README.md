# Workshop: combining segment and persona (mini-case)

THEORETICAL RECAP Before diving into the workshop, let's consolidate the essential concepts you've been building over the last few activities. A market segment is a group of individuals sharing measurable, homogeneous characteristics (demographic, behavioral, psychographic, or geographic) that make them respond similarly to a marketing stimulus. Segmentation is the analytical foundation: it tells you who the broad groups are. A persona is a semi-fictional, humanized representation of one specific sub-type within a segment. Where a segment says 'urban professionals aged 28–40 with a high income,' a persona says 'Sophie, 34, UX designer in Lyon, who browses on mobile during her commute and needs quick, trustworthy information before purchasing.' The persona gives the segment a face, a voice, and a decision-making logic. The critical mental model: segmentation → persona → message. You cannot build a credible persona without a well-defined segment, and you cannot craft relevant communication without a credible persona. The two tools are complementary, not interchangeable. Common mistakes to avoid: creating a persona that does not clearly belong to any defined segment; building a segment so broad that multiple contradictory personas could emerge from it; confusing demographic data (segment-level) with motivations and pain points (persona-level); and validating a persona only with assumptions rather than real or simulated qualitative data. Mini example: a fitness app targets 'sedentary adults aged 30–50 living in urban areas' (segment). From this, two personas emerge — Marcus, a 42-year-old manager who lacks time and needs 10-minute routines, and Léa, a 35-year-old teacher motivated by community...
