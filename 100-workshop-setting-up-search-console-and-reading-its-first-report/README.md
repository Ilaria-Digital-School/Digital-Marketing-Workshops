# Workshop: Setting up Search Console and reading its first report

THEORETICAL RECAP Google Search Console (GSC) is a free tool from Google that reports how a website performs in organic search. Unlike Google Analytics, which tracks user behavior once someone lands on your site, GSC focuses on the acquisition side: what queries bring impressions, which pages get clicked, your average position, and whether Google can crawl and index your pages correctly. The right mental model: GSC is the bridge between your SEO work (on-page, netlinking, technical fixes like the 404s and 301 redirects you studied) and Google's actual perception of your site. Four core metrics drive the Performance report: Impressions (how often your pages appeared in results), Clicks (how many times users clicked), CTR (Clicks divided by Impressions), and Average Position (your mean ranking for a query). A common beginner mistake is confusing impressions with visits, or reading a high average position as "success" without checking whether that position generates clicks. Another frequent error is ignoring property verification, which blocks all data. Mini example: a page ranks at position 8.2 with 5,000 impressions but only 40 clicks (0.8% CTR). This signals a visibility problem, not a ranking one, likely a weak title tag or meta description. That single insight can guide a concrete optimization. This is exactly the diagnostic reflex this workshop builds. ---- WORKSHOP BRIEF The goal of this workshop is to move from theory to practice by configuring Google Search Console on a real website and producing a short diagnostic report based on its first data.
