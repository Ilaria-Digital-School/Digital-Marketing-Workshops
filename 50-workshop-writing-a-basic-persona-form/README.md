# Workshop: writing a basic persona form

THEORETICAL RECAP A persona is a fictional but realistic representation of a segment of your target audience. It is built from real data — market research, customer interviews, behavioral analytics, CRM data — and synthesized into a humanized profile that helps marketing teams make concrete decisions. Unlike a raw segment (e.g., 'women aged 25–34 interested in fitness'), a persona gives that segment a name, a face, a context, motivations, frustrations, and goals. This makes it actionable: instead of targeting an abstraction, you address a person. The mental model to keep in mind: a persona lives at the intersection of demographic data (who they are), psychographic data (what drives them), and behavioral data (what they do). A good persona answers three questions — What does this person want? What stops them from getting it? How does your product or service fit into their life? Common mistakes to avoid: making the persona too vague ('likes social media, aged 20–50'), inventing data without any grounding in real insights, creating too many personas at once, or confusing the persona with the product target. A persona is not a wish list — it reflects reality. Mini example: 'Sophie, 32, junior project manager in a mid-sized company, uses LinkedIn daily, wants to grow professionally but struggles to find time for training. She is looking for short, flexible online learning formats she can access on her commute.' This workshop builds directly on what you have learned about segmentation criteria and persona definitions.
