# Workshop: enriching a course with behavioral data

**Theoretical Recap** Behavioral data refers to all quantitative and qualitative information collected about how users actually interact with a product, service, or digital channel. Unlike demographic data (age, location, job title), behavioral data captures what users do: pages visited, time spent, clicks, drop-off points, purchase frequency, content consumed, and more. When integrated into a customer journey map or a persona, behavioral data transforms abstract archetypes into evidence-based profiles that reflect real patterns of use. A common mistake at this stage is confusing declared data (what users say they do, gathered via surveys or interviews) with behavioral data (what they actually do, gathered via analytics tools, heatmaps, CRM logs, or session recordings). Both are valuable, but conflating them leads to inaccurate personas and misleading journey maps. The key mental model here is the following: behavioral data should always be used to challenge, validate, or enrich what you believe you know about your audience. For example, imagine you built a persona named "Laura, 34, marketing manager" based on interviews. You assumed she discovers new tools mainly through LinkedIn. But web analytics reveal that 62% of users matching her profile arrive via Google Search, not social media. This single behavioral insight forces you to revise Laura's touchpoints and reshape the awareness stage of her journey entirely.
