# Workshop: Weekly vs monthly monitoring plan

**Theoretical Recap** Monitoring frequency is not a cosmetic choice — it directly determines your ability to act on data in time. A weekly monitoring plan focuses on operational, short-cycle KPIs: metrics that fluctuate rapidly and require fast corrective action, such as click-through rates, daily traffic, conversion rates on active campaigns, or cost-per-click. A monthly monitoring plan is reserved for strategic, slower-moving KPIs: customer acquisition cost (CAC), monthly recurring revenue (MRR), organic traffic trends, or email list growth. The core mental model is simple — match the monitoring frequency to the decision cycle the KPI informs. If a KPI drives a decision you need to make this week, monitor it weekly. If it drives a quarterly budget or strategic pivot, monthly is sufficient. Common mistakes include monitoring everything weekly (which creates noise and alert fatigue) or monitoring everything monthly (which means you react too late to operational problems). Another frequent error is confusing leading and lagging indicators when assigning frequency — leading indicators typically need higher-frequency tracking. A mini example: for an e-commerce site running paid ads, 'daily ad spend vs. budget' is monitored weekly or even daily, while 'customer lifetime value' is reviewed monthly or quarterly.
