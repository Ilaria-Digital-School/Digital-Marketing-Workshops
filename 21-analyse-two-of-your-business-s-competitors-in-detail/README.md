# Analyse two of your business's competitors in detail

Theoretical Recap Before diving into the analysis, let's clarify the core concepts. A competitor analysis is a structured assessment of competing businesses, focusing on their potential to capture your target market share or affect your profitability. Its purpose is to identify their strengths and weaknesses relative to your own business. A clear mental model for this analysis is a direct comparison based on key business dimensions. You should examine elements like the competitor's product or service offering, pricing model, distribution channels, marketing messages, and target audience. The goal is to move beyond a simple list and identify actionable insights for your own strategy. A common mistake is to confuse indirect competitors and substitute products. An indirect competitor operates in a different product category but competes for the same consumer need and budget. A substitute product serves a similar function through a different method. Another error is focusing too much on what competitors do poorly, without truly understanding what they do well that earns them customers. Consider a mini example: a local café analysing another café. A direct competitor analysis would assess its coffee quality, price per cup, and ambiance. An analysis of an indirect competitor like a tea shop would focus on how it satisfies the same need for a casual, in-person social drink. A substitute analysis might look at instant coffee machines sold in supermarkets, which provide a similar function at home. Workshop Brief This workshop puts theory into practice.
