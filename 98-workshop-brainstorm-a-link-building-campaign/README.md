# Workshop: brainstorm a link building campaign

THEORETICAL RECAP Link building is the process of acquiring hyperlinks from other websites to your own. In SEO, backlinks act as votes of confidence: search engines interpret a link from an authoritative site as a signal that your content is trustworthy and relevant. The core mental model is simple — think of the web as a network of recommendations. Each quality link passes 'authority' (often called link equity) toward your page, boosting its potential to rank. But not all links are equal: relevance, authority of the linking domain, anchor text, and placement all matter far more than raw quantity. A useful framework distinguishes three pillars: (1) the target — which pages need links and for which keywords; (2) the asset — the content or reason someone would want to link to you (a guide, study, tool, infographic); (3) the outreach — how you identify and contact prospects. Common mistakes include chasing volume over quality, buying links or using link farms (which risk penalties), ignoring topical relevance, over-optimizing anchor text, and having no linkable asset to offer. Mini example: A cooking blog publishes an original data study on 'grocery inflation and home cooking habits'. They then contact food journalists and nutrition bloggers, who cite the study and link back. This is ethical, relevance-driven link building — an asset attracts editorially given links.
