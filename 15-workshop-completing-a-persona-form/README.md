# Workshop: completing a persona form

**Theoretical Recap** A persona (also called a buyer persona) is a semi-fictional representation of your ideal customer, built from real data, behavioral observations, and reasonable assumptions. It is not just a demographic profile — it is a narrative tool that helps you understand who you are talking to, what motivates them, what obstacles they face, and how they make decisions. A well-constructed persona directly informs your content strategy, your channel choices, your messaging, and ultimately your conversion funnel. The key elements of a persona are: a name and humanizing details (age, job, location), motivations and goals (what do they want to achieve?), frustrations and pain points (what gets in their way?), digital behavior (which platforms do they use, when, and how?), and decision triggers (what convinces them to act?). A common mistake is confusing a persona with a target audience segment. A segment is a group; a persona is one specific individual representing that group. Another frequent error is filling out a persona form with invented data rather than grounding it in real observations — always try to anchor your persona in at least some real signals, even if you are working from hypothesis at this stage.
