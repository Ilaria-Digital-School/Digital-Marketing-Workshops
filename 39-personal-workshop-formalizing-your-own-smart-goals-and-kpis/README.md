# Personal workshop: Formalizing your own SMART goals and KPIs

THEORETICAL RECAP A SMART goal is a goal that meets five criteria: Specific (clearly defined, no ambiguity), Measurable (quantifiable with a concrete indicator), Achievable (realistic given your resources), Relevant (aligned with your broader strategy), and Time-bound (with a clear deadline). A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) is the metric you use to track progress toward that goal — it is not the goal itself. The mental model to keep in mind: the goal defines WHERE you want to go, the KPI tells you HOW FAR you've come, and the baseline tells you WHERE you started. Common mistakes to avoid: setting vague goals ('increase traffic' instead of 'increase organic traffic by 20%'), confusing a KPI with a vanity metric (page views without context), forgetting to set a baseline before launching any action, or choosing a KPI that is impossible to measure with the tools you actually have access to. A mini example: instead of 'I want more newsletter subscribers,' a correct SMART formulation would be — 'Increase the newsletter subscriber base from 1,200 to 1,800 contacts via a lead magnet campaign on LinkedIn, by the end of Q3 2025, tracked weekly via Mailchimp subscriber count.' The KPI here is the subscriber count, the baseline is 1,200, and the target is 1,800. WORKSHOP BRIEF This workshop asks you to apply everything you have learned throughout this chapter by formalizing your own SMART goals and their associated KPIs in a real or realistic professional context.
