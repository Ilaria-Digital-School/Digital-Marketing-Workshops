# Workshop: Identifying leading indicators for a given objective

THEORETICAL RECAP Before diving into the workshop, let's consolidate the key concept you explored in the previous lesson on leading vs. lagging KPIs — because this distinction is at the heart of everything you are about to do. A lagging indicator measures an outcome that has already happened. Revenue, churn rate, number of conversions: these tell you how well you performed, but they cannot tell you what to do next in time to change the result. A leading indicator, by contrast, is a predictive signal — it measures activity or behavior that, if tracked and acted upon, allows you to influence the final outcome before it materializes. For example, if your lagging KPI is 'Monthly Recurring Revenue,' a leading indicator could be 'number of free trial activations per week,' because trial activations today predict revenue in 30 days. The critical mental model to retain: leading indicators sit earlier in the funnel than lagging ones. They are actionable, measurable in near real-time, and causally linked to the outcome you want. The most common mistake learners make is confusing a leading indicator with any early-funnel metric. Not all early metrics are predictive. A leading indicator must have a demonstrated or logical causal relationship with the target outcome. Mini example: Objective — increase blog-to-demo conversion rate by 20% in 90 days (lagging KPI: demo request rate).
